Sentinel 1.10.0 Argus: chat safety for Roblox and Discord, and smoother everything

Chat safety (new, Windows app)
• Sentinel can now watch Roblox and the Discord app for scams and for people who may not be safe to talk to: someone asking a child to keep secrets, to say where they live or go to school, to send pictures, to move to another app, or to meet; threats to share pictures; free Robux, V-Bucks and Nitro; requests for a password or a code; "you go first" trades. A warning appears right beside the message, with what to do.
• It reads the game or server a chat is in, so ordinary talk is left alone: "where do you live" in a role-play town is about a house in the game, and fighting talk in a shooter is just the game. One ordinary question is never enough to flag someone; signs add up per person.
• Everything happens on your computer. No message, name or game leaves it, and nothing is saved or logged. Sentinel never changes Roblox or Discord, never puts code into them, and never types or clicks in them.
• It never gets in the way of a game: nothing is read while a game's chat is closed, the Sentinel badge shows in Roblox only when you open the Esc menu, and warnings sit small beside the chat box. No gold line crosses a game.
• It is off until you, or a parent, turn it on: the app asks once, and the switch is in Live protection.

Live scanning
• Marks follow the page as it scrolls, by the page's own pixels, whether you use a wheel, a touchpad or the scrollbar: they no longer stutter between notches or step aside while a touchpad scrolls.
• A safe result now gets a green mask, as the website shows, instead of a tick.
• Marks sit centred beside the link.
• Apps installed from Edge or Chrome, such as Instagram, are no longer mistaken for a browser.

Website
• Much lighter on phones and computers: every 3D mask is drawn by one renderer instead of one each (seven on the home page), idle drifting pauses while you scroll, and masks are prepared in quiet moments. On the test computer, scrolling the home page went from 8 to 33 frames a second.
• The hero mask no longer spins to follow a finger on a phone, turns calmly toward the mouse on a computer, is a darker silver, and its eyes always glow. The still picture and the 3D mask now line up, so the mask no longer jumps when the 3D one takes over.
• The scope's ring now lights as the black hole appears.
• Gold lettering catches the light of your pointer, and clicking a tab now sights its chapter through a scope.
• The tab title reads "Sentinel: Unmask the Internet".
