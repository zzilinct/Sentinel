Sentinel 1.8.9: three masks in 3D, and a website that moves

**Design**
• The three masks are rendered in 3D behind the home, pricing and download pages, from the same drawings as the icons: the scam mask in polished gold, the malware mask in candy-red metal, the virus mask in burnt orange with its spores orbiting it.
• They regroup for each section as you scroll: around the headline, peeking past the demo, one stepping forward for each colour of the manifesto, circling the game and the security rings, rising behind the closing card. They turn to watch your pointer.
• A slow nebula behind them takes its colours from the section you are reading. Panels are frosted glass, so the masks show through as coloured light.
• Every section arrives its own way: cards flip up, swing in from the sides, fall like dominoes, open through an iris, tilt up from the floor, split open like doors, or zoom in from a blur.
• Clicking a tab sweeps three panels in the masks' colours over the page, names the chapter, and lands you there with everything already shown. A pill glides between the tabs, a chapter rail runs down the left edge on wide screens, and a bar along the top shows how far you have read.
• The home page is reordered to read as a story: the masks, where they appear, what the colours mean, how a verdict is made, then the game, a link to try, plans, your data and questions. Nothing was removed.
• Light theme, phones, reduced motion and browsers without WebGL are all covered: without WebGL the flat masks stay, and with reduced motion nothing moves.
