Sentinel 1.9.8 Argus: pages about a company are no longer mistaken for it

• Reviews, news articles, how-to guides, forums and store listings that name a company in their address (a Chase review on NerdWallet, trustpilot.com/review/www.chase.com, a PCMag guide to closing a PayPal account) are no longer marked as pretending to be that company. These could show up yellow when live scanning search results.
• An address ending in a website's name (…/review/www.chase.com) is no longer mistaken for a .com program download.
• Places named for a sponsor (wellsfargocenterphilly.com, the arena) are left alone, and a brand glued to words that hide a lure (paypalcentersupport) is still caught.
• A bank's name glued to "bank" or "login" in a folder beside a sign-in folder (…/login/chasebank/…) is caught, and so are pages on names Netlify made up for sites their owners never named.
• Measured on today's public phishing lists: 287 of 300 phishing addresses flagged (95.7%, up from 94.7%). All 111 real sites with brand-like names are left alone (110 before), and the 99 real pages and 127 real sites Sentinel is tested against show no false alarms.
