Sentinel 1.8.5: email scams caught even when they have no link

**Email protection**
• Scams that only need you to call, reply or pay are now caught: "your renewal was charged, call this number to cancel", "your computer is infected, call Microsoft", the boss who needs gift cards or a confidential wire today, "reply with your Social Security number and bank details", unpaid toll and DMV fine demands, money for a stranger's plane ticket, and unclaimed inheritances.
• Web pages sent as attachments (fake invoices and voicemails ending in .html or .htm) are treated as phishing.
• Real mail stays unmarked: a bank's "did you make this purchase? call us" alert, receipts that mention another company, and mail from companies' own sending domains (Dropbox, Facebook, Gmail) are no longer mistaken for impostors.
• Fixed: a message from any Gmail or Outlook.com address was treated as if Google or Microsoft had sent it, which softened the checks on scammers writing from free mailboxes.
• "Bank of America", "Wells Fargo" and other names written as several words are recognised as the brands they are.

**Measured**
• A new set of 80 real emails from real companies and 46 of today's scams. Full emails: all 46 scams flagged (was 28), and none of the 80 real emails (was 3 false alarms).
• Inbox rows, where Sentinel sees only the sender's name and a line of text: 14 of 46 scams flagged (was 4), still with no false alarms. Sentinel stays careful there on purpose: without the sender's address it will not guess.
