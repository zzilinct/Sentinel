    IIIThe masks

    Three masks, one glance

    Every threat gets its own face, and every face gets a colour. Yellow means look closer. Orange means it’s probably dangerous. Red means Sentinel has proof.

    III

    Scam

    Sites built to take your money, passwords or wallet.

    • Look-alike and misspelled brand domains
    • Fake login, parcel-fee and prize pages
    • Crypto drainers asking for recovery phrases
    IV

    Virus

    Files that infect your computer the moment they run.

    • Programs disguised as PDFs and photos
    • Known malicious files, matched by fingerprint
    • Archives smuggling executables
    V

    Malware

    Pages and documents that attack from the inside.

    • Fake browser updates and “paste this command” lures
    • Hidden cryptominers and invisible frames
    • Macro documents and download-and-run scripts

    What Sentinel tells apart

    A mask says which family and how sure Sentinel is. The result also names the specific trick, from the evidence it found, so you know what you are looking at.

    Fake login page

    Asks for a password or card on a site that is not the brand.

    Brand impersonation

    Borrows a name it does not own, in the address or on the page.

    Crypto wallet drainer

    Wants a recovery phrase, or promises to double what you send.

    Parcel fee lure

    A held package that clears for a small fee and your card details.

    Tech support scam

    A fake virus alert with a number to call.

    Prize or giveaway bait

    You have won something you never entered.

    Fake online store

    Deep discounts, no refund policy, contact only on WhatsApp.

    Fake government service

    Tax refunds and fines from a site the government does not run.

    Program disguised as a document

    Invoice.pdf.exe is a program, whatever its icon says.

    Macro-enabled document

    A document that runs code the moment you enable editing.

    Archive hiding a program

    A zip that opens to an executable.

    Unexpected program download

    A link or page that pushes an installer at you.

    Fake browser update

    Your browser is fine. The update is the payload.

    Paste-this-command lure

    A fake check that asks you to run a command yourself.

    Hidden cryptominer

    A page quietly mining with your computer.

    Hidden or obfuscated code

    Invisible frames and scripts that hide what they do.

    XIYour data

    What Sentinel keeps, and what it never does

    Addresses, not content

    Sentinel checks where a link goes. It never records what you type or reads pages behind your logins.

    Two-factor sign-in

    Authenticator-app codes, lockout after repeated failures, and a list of every signed-in device you can revoke.

    Research happens on our side

    Suspicious pages are opened by Sentinel’s servers, which refuse to reach private networks, never by your computer.

    Downloads stay on your computer

    The Sentinel app analyses them where they are. Only a SHA-256 fingerprint is compared online. A file you check on the website is uploaded for that check and not kept.

    XIIQuestions

    Good to know

    What’s the difference between yellow, orange and red?
    Yellow means a few warning signs, worth a second look. Orange means many strong signs at once, so it’s probably dangerous. Red is only ever shown with evidence: the site is on a live threat list, matches a known scam kit while impersonating a brand, or does something unmistakably hostile.
    Do I need to download anything?
    No. The web app works in any browser for scanning links and files. Live protection, meaning masks on search results, emails and downloads, runs through the Sentinel app, which you download once.
    What does “research” mean on Pro, Max and Ultimate?
    Sentinel visits the site from its own servers and gathers facts the address alone can’t reveal: when the domain was registered, its certificate, where it redirects, whether its forms send passwords elsewhere, and whether its scripts try to mine crypto or push downloads.
    How are live scanning hours counted?
    Only minutes in which Sentinel actually checks something for you count, and each minute counts once however many results it checks. Hours reset every Monday.
    Does Sentinel need an AI account?
    No. Every verdict comes from Sentinel’s own threat intelligence, checklist and research. Connecting ChatGPT, Claude, Gemini or DeepSeek is an optional extra for asking questions about a link.
