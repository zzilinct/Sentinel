3. What Sentinel does, and what it cannot do

Sentinel looks at links, emails and files and tells you how risky they appear and why. It checks known-threat lists, runs a checklist of warning signs, compares against known scam patterns and, on some plans, fetches and inspects the page from our servers. It then shows you a colour-coded mask and the reasons behind it.

Sentinel reduces risk. It cannot remove it. Specifically:

  • A link, email or file with no mask is not guaranteed to be safe. New scams appear faster than any list can track.
  • A mask is Sentinel’s assessment of warning signs. It is not proof of wrongdoing by any person or business, and it is not a legal finding.
  • Labels such as “Possible virus” or “Likely malware” describe the evidence Sentinel found. They are not a substitute for antivirus software that inspects your whole computer.
  • Sentinel does not stop you visiting a site, opening a file or entering your details. The decision, and its consequences, stay with you.

5. Acceptable use

You may use Sentinel to check things you are about to open, and to protect yourself and people you are responsible for. You may not:

  • Use Sentinel to probe, attack, overload or scrape other people’s systems, or to test whether your own scam pages get past it.
  • File reports about sites you do not genuinely believe are harmful. Three reports from separate accounts can mark a site as confirmed for everyone, so false reports harm real people.

  • Resell access or use Sentinel to build a competing threat feed without our written agreement.

We can suspend or close accounts that break these rules, and we can remove reports that turn out to be false.

7. The desktop app and browser companion

The desktop app runs a copy of Sentinel on your own computer and keeps its data there. It can watch your Downloads folder, check new files against known threats, and move files it flags into a quarantine folder that you control. Quarantine moves a file; it does not delete it, and you can restore it.

On Windows, the app can also read the web address of the page your browser is currently showing, using the accessibility interface Windows provides for that purpose, and warn you if that address is dangerous. It reads the address only: not the contents of the page, not form fields, and nothing you type. It tells you which browsers are installed and which are running, so it can offer the right companion build. This is off if you turn it off, and the app works without it.

The browser companion adds masks to search results and email in Chrome, Edge, Brave and Firefox. Every part of this can be turned off at any time.

The app checks for new versions and installs them itself, so you do not have to download Sentinel again. Updates come from Sentinel's own release page and are verified against a checksum before they are applied.

The Windows installer is not yet code-signed, so Windows will show a warning on first run. Each release publishes SHA-256 checksums so you can verify what you downloaded.

8. Optional AI assistants
