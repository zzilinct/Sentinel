Release notes

Sentinel 1.8.7

Fixes from a full review of the last six updates: hidden PowerShell downloaders and phishing forms with little scripts in them are caught again, real security mail stays unmarked, marks come back after a wheel turned over something that does not scroll, and Restore keeps a backup of anything it cannot put back.

1.8.6: the file scanner leaves your real programs alone: measured on 4,500 real files from Windows and the programs installed with it, none is marked. Scripts are only treated as downloaders when they both fetch and run something, and a program or archive hiding behind a document name is still caught.

1.8.5: email scams are caught even when they have no link: callback scams (“call this number to cancel”), fake tech support, gift-card and wire requests from a “boss”, requests for your bank details or Social Security number, and toll and fine demands. On 80 real emails from real companies and 46 of today’s scams, every scam was flagged and no real email was.

1.8.4: more phishing caught from the address alone (96.3% of today’s phishing addresses, up from 95.0%): pages hidden behind generated folder names, throwaway site-builder pages and web hosts’ temporary addresses are now flagged. Shared Google documents are no longer marked yellow, and Sentinel is now measured against 99 real pages deep inside real sites, with no false alarms.

1.8.3: Sentinel is gentler on your PC. It only listens to your mouse while live scanning has marks on screen, never starts an update over a fullscreen game, and asks Microsoft Defender before acting on a file it cannot read. Restore now brings back Startup shortcuts and scheduled tasks too, and never overwrites a newer file.

1.8.2: marks stay on their results when you keep scrolling at the end of a page or scroll an inbox list, and a slow browser no longer throws them back mid-scroll. Accounts are safer: Google sign-in finishes only in the browser that started it, turning on two-factor asks for your password, and community reports need confirmed, week-old accounts. Light mode is readable everywhere, every page links the Privacy Policy, Terms and Refund Policy, and the browser companion (1.4.1) matches the website.

1.8.1: marks move the moment your mouse wheel turns the moment your mouse wheel turns, along the same curve as the page, and positions read mid-scroll no longer throw them off the page. Sentinel reads each result’s title and your search words to understand a site without opening it, and reads names made of real words as words, so overdrive.com is no longer mistaken for OneDrive while look-alikes such as onedrlve are still caught.

The website has light and dark themes, new fonts made for reading, a Refund Policy, and clearer Privacy and Terms. Other browser extensions can no longer read your account through the site.

1.7.0 brought together the 1.6.11 to 1.6.18 updates: Google results and ads checked, inbox scams caught, downloads checked in the background, much less work in the background, and live scanning tested in Edge, Chrome and Firefox.

Set up in a few minutes

01

Install Sentinel

Run the installer. Sentinel opens and adds its mask to your system tray. Live scanning stays off until you start it in step 03.

02

No sign-up needed

The app gives your computer its own account the first time it starts, so it protects you without signing in. Download protection and Defense come with Pro, Max and Ultimate. You can sign in with your own account later if you want your history on other devices.

03

Press Start scanning

One button in Live protection, or Scan with beside the browser you use. A gold line crosses your screen, and when the tint fades Sentinel is ready. There is no add-on to install.

04

Browse normally

A gold mask in the corner of your browser shows Sentinel is watching. Search, and a mask appears beside every result. It only works while the browser is in front of you, and private windows leave no trace in Sentinel.
