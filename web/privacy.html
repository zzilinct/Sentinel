Privacy

Addresses, not your life.

A protection product should collect as little as possible. Here is exactly what Sentinel sees.

Last updated 16 September 2026 · beta

What Sentinel processes

  • Web addresses of search results, pages you open and links you scan, so they can be checked.
  • Page contents of a site, fetched by our servers only when a scan includes research (Pro and Max). Nothing from your own browser session is sent — no cookies, no logged-in pages.
  • Email details your mail client already shows — sender, reply-to, subject, a text excerpt, link addresses and attachment names — when email protection or a paste-in email scan is used. They are analysed in memory and not stored.
  • Files you upload to the virus & malware scanner, inspected in memory and discarded. Only the SHA-256 fingerprint may be kept.
  • Downloads on your computer are inspected locally by the Sentinel app. Only the fingerprint is sent to check against known threats.
  • Your account: email, name, a scrypt password hash, plan, weekly usage counters, scan history (90 days), trusted sites, reports, and a security log of sign-ins (180 days).

What Sentinel never does

  • Record what you type into websites, or read pages behind your logins.
  • Sell or share personal data for advertising.
  • Ask for passwords to other services. The optional AI assistants use API keys you create, stored with AES-256-GCM encryption.

Where information comes from

Threat knowledge comes from Sentinel’s own database, community reports, and public feeds including OpenPhish, URLhaus (abuse.ch) and Phishing.Database. If configured, links are also checked against Google Safe Browsing. Registration details come from public RDAP registries.

Security

Traffic is encrypted with TLS. Sessions use HttpOnly, SameSite cookies; passwords are hashed with scrypt; accounts lock after repeated failed sign-ins; two-factor authentication is available to everyone. Research requests run from isolated servers that refuse to connect to private networks.

Your choices

You can delete your account at any time from Security settings, revoke any signed-in device, and remove trusted sites. Deleting your account removes your history, reports, usage and connected keys.
